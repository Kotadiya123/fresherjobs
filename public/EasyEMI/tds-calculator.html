What is TDS?

TDS or Tax Deducted at Source is a process implemented by the Indian Government, which is collected from salary, rent, commission etc. The person who is paying the amount should deduct TDS from it. TDS should be deducted irrespective of the made of payment cash, cheque credit and both the account should be linked with PAN. The purpose of TDS may have been to reduce the chances of tax evasion by the recipient of the income. But, for an honest taxpayer, it also brings a few benefits.

How to calculate TDS?

The formula for average income tax rate:

Average Income tax rate = Income tax payable as per the slab rates / Total estimated income for the financial year

For example,

If Mr Ramesh has received the salary of ₹1,00,000 in a year (these rates will change with every financial year).

According to Section 192, the TDS on your salary based on the current year's slab rates would be Rs. 1,42,500. After the addition of 4% education and higher education cess, the net payable tax becomes Rs. 1,48,200.

Average Income tax rate = Rs. 1,48,200/12,00,000*100
= 12.35%

So, the TDS deducted from Mr Ramesh's salary each month would be 12.35% of 1 lakh, which is,
= Rs. 12,350
